FAQ

Questions, answered honestly

Is ElevenLabs worth it in 2026?

If you publish audio regularly — YouTube, podcasts, courses, audiobooks — yes: it replaces several subscriptions (TTS, cloning, dubbing, SFX, music) with one credit pool, and its realism is widely regarded as best-in-class. If you only need a few minutes of basic speech a month, the free tier or a cheaper single-purpose tool may be enough.

Is there a free plan?

Yes — 10,000 credits per month (about 10 minutes of speech) with access to TTS, sound effects and voice design. It requires attribution and doesn't include a commercial license.

How does pricing and the credit system work?

Every plan includes a monthly pool of credits shared across all products. Roughly, one text character costs one credit on the flagship model, with discounted rates on faster models. If you run out, you can buy overage minutes, with rates that drop on higher plans. Annual billing works out to about two months free.

Is voice cloning legal?

Cloning your own voice, or a voice you have explicit written permission to clone, is legal in most places. Cloning someone without consent can violate ElevenLabs' terms and laws on impersonation, publicity rights and fraud. ElevenLabs requires consent verification, and you should always disclose AI narration where your platform or jurisdiction requires it.

What's the difference between Instant and Professional Voice Cloning?

Instant Voice Cloning (Starter plan and up) builds a usable clone from a few minutes of audio. Professional Voice Cloning (Creator plan and up) trains on longer samples for near-indistinguishable quality — it's the feature most serious creators upgrade for.

How accurate is the dubbing?

Very good for clear, single-speaker content, and it preserves the original voice's character across languages. Fast overlapping speech, heavy slang and niche jargon still benefit from a human review pass — the dubbing studio lets you edit the translation before export.

What languages are supported?

90+ on Eleven v4, the flagship model released September 28, 2026, including Swahili, Amharic, Somali, Luganda, Kamba, Shona and Zulu, across TTS and dubbing. Check the live language list for your specific target market.

Does Lumora Voices cover Eleven v4?

Yes — we voiced a whole short film with Eleven v4 on the $6 Starter plan and scored it 8/10. Our full Eleven v4 review covers what it gets right, where it falls short, pricing, and v4 vs v4 Turbo.

Can I create audiobooks with it?

Yes. Long-form projects are a core use case: consistent narration across chapters, pronunciation controls, and regeneration of individual sentences instead of re-recording sessions. Note that some retail platforms have their own rules about AI-narrated titles, so check distributor policies.

What are AI agents?

Conversational voice assistants that combine speech recognition, an LLM and ElevenLabs voices. Businesses use them for phone support, bookings and lead qualification; developers embed them in products. Agent plans are billed by call minutes, from a free prototyping tier to thousands of minutes for teams.

Can I generate music, and can I use it commercially?

Yes, Eleven Music generates original tracks from text prompts. Commercial usage rights depend on your plan and the current license terms, so review those before using tracks in monetized or client work.

Can I create sound effects?

Yes — describe the sound in plain text ("rain on a tin roof, distant thunder") and generate custom SFX for videos, games and podcasts. It draws from the same credit pool as everything else.

Can businesses and agencies use ElevenLabs?

Yes. Commercial licensing starts on the $6 Starter plan, Scale and Business plans add seats and volume, and Enterprise adds custom contracts, security and support. Agencies commonly run client voiceover, localization and IVR work through it.

What industries use it?

Media and YouTube production, podcasting, publishing and audiobooks, e-learning and education, gaming, marketing agencies, customer support, healthcare communication, and accessibility tooling are the most common.

Do I need to be technical to use it?

No. The web studio is point-and-click: type text, pick a voice, generate. The API exists for developers who want to automate, but it's optional.

What are the honest downsides?

The credit system takes a little learning; heavy long-form usage can get expensive versus flat-rate tools; the best cloning quality sits behind paid plans; and generated audio still benefits from human review for pronunciation of names and niche terms.
